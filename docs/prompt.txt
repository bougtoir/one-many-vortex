HYPOTHESES TO TEST / REFINE

Do not preserve any hypothesis if literature review invalidates it.

H1 — Interpretive-Distance Hypothesis

Greater distance between newly emerging problem domains and the problem environment under which inherited norms were stabilized should increase interpretive load, other things equal.

H2 — Authority-Extension Hypothesis

The broader the range of historically novel domains over which the same divine authority retains jurisdiction, the greater the institutional importance of authorized interpretation.

H3 — Novelty-Induced Differentiation Hypothesis

Where inherited authority underdetermines responses to historically novel problems, novelty should create opportunities for interpretive multiplication even under ontological unity.

H4 — Conditional Reintegration Hypothesis

Interpretive multiplication should be more likely to elicit formal reintegration where exclusivity, interpretive centralization, and institutional incentives for doctrinal uniformity are jointly high.

H5 — Authority-Scope-Release Hypothesis

Where norms can remain socially binding while becoming less dependent on case-specific divine adjudication, novel problem domains may permit wider normative adaptation without equivalent expansion of authorized interpretation.

H6 — Accretion–Plurality Hypothesis

Interpretive accumulation may simultaneously stabilize a tradition and increase the number of internally defensible interpretive pathways.

H7 — Vortex-Recurrence Hypothesis

Historical sequences should exhibit recurrent movements toward aggregation and differentiation, but successive states should remain historically non-identical because each transition changes the institutional and interpretive state space.

COVER LETTER POSITIONING

Do NOT pitch the article merely as:

“a new model of monotheism and polytheism.”

Instead pitch it as:

a general theory of how unity and plurality in divine representation can be generated, reversed, and regenerated as historical environments change.

The strongest hook is:

Many→One has often been treated as development.

This paper asks why directional meaning should be inferred from aggregation itself when the reverse process is equally formally describable as differentiation.

It then shows that historical novelty supplies a mechanism by which apparently unified authority may repeatedly generate interpretive plurality.

Finally, it introduces authority-scope release as an alternative to indefinite expansion of divine jurisdiction.

⸻

FINAL THEORY STATEMENT

The final manuscript should, if supported by the evidence, converge toward a statement approximately like:

The One–Many distinction is better treated, in part, as a dynamic problem of representational architecture than as a developmental ladder. Divine functions, attributes, narratives, and interpretive authority may be aggregated, differentiated, and reaggregated across historical time. Because changing worlds continually generate problems not contained in earlier normative environments, ontological unity can produce interpretive plurality, which institutions may accommodate or recompress. Yet perpetual authority extension is not the only possibility: a divine authority may remain sacred while relinquishing regulatory functions whose historical work has been completed. The resulting system is not a cycle returning to its origin but a path-dependent vortex in which One and Many repeatedly reappear in transformed states.

FINAL SUCCESS CRITERION

The manuscript succeeds only if a skeptical reader can see that it contributes something more specific than:

“religions change over time,”

“monotheism and polytheism are complicated,”

or

“religions reinterpret old teachings.”

The paper must demonstrate that:

1. One/Many can be modeled as aggregation–differentiation dynamics
2. developmental direction can be challenged by a Directional Symmetry Test
3. historical novelty provides a mechanism for renewed interpretive differentiation
4. authorized interpretation links divine continuity to institutional structure
5. authority-scope release supplies a theoretically distinct alternative to indefinite divine jurisdiction
6. the complete system generates falsifiable historical and comparative predictions

That integrated theory is the scientific contribution of Paper 3/3.
