# Journal Selection Gate — Paper 3

Candidates compared on scope, tolerance for conceptual/theoretical articles, comparative-religion fit, citation ecology, formal-model tolerance, and length/APC considerations.

| Journal | Fit assessment |
|---|---|
| **Method & Theory in the Study of Religion (Brill, NAASR)** | Scope is literally "problems of methodology and theory in the academic study of religion," including category critique and the history of the discipline. Bibliographic neighborhood match is the strongest available: Barrett's "Theological Correctness" (1999) and the Masuzawa/Invention-of-World-Religions symposium (2008) appeared here; category-level critiques of monotheism/polytheism are its home turf. Hybrid journal; standard article length accommodates a long theoretical argument. **Selected.** |
| Journal of the American Academy of Religion (OUP) | Top field journal; accepts theory but favors articles anchored in specific historical/ethnographic material; a fully conceptual formal-model article is a harder sell; also a poor citation-ecology match for the specialized method debates this paper must answer to. Runner-up. |
| Religion (Taylor & Francis) | Publishes theory and comparativist work; recently carried the mono-/multi-devotionalism critique (2024). Credible alternative; slightly weaker match on method-level formalization. |
| Numen (Brill, IAHR) | Strong for history-of-religions and "theoretical innovations," but emphasizes empirical source-based research; a model-building article is admissible but less central. Backup if MTSR declines. |

**Decision: Method & Theory in the Study of Religion (MTSR).**

Rationale: the paper's primary interlocutors — teleology critique, category construction, theological incorrectness, the mono/poly binary debate — are most densely located in the MTSR/NAASR conversation. Citation ecology is decisive, not prestige.

Citation style: MTSR uses author–date (author year: page) with a reference list; ~10,000-word ceiling is typical for full articles — the manuscript targets ~9,000–10,000 words.
